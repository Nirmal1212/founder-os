# Launch tiers — match effort to impact

The most common launch mistake is treating every release the same — either over-producing a minor update or under-producing a major one. Tier first, then resource accordingly.

## The three tiers
**Tier 1 — Major moment** (new product, category bet, big funding/partnership)
- Full coordinated launch: founder narrative, press/PR, multi-channel same-day, possibly an event.
- Weeks of prep, a hard date, pre-briefed press, a warmed audience.
- Rare — a few per year at most. Over-using "tier 1" devalues it.

**Tier 2 — Notable feature / meaningful update**
- Blog post + email to list + social + maybe a Product Hunt or community push.
- Days of prep, lighter coordination. The workhorse launch.

**Tier 3 — Incremental**
- Changelog entry, in-app announcement, one social post.
- Minimal prep. Most releases live here — and that's correct.

## How to tier (ask)
- Is this new-to-world for us, or an improvement? (New → higher tier.)
- Does it change who we can sell to or what we are? (Yes → higher.)
- Will the target audience genuinely care, or only existing power users? (Broad → higher.)
- Do we have proof/readiness to withstand attention? (No → lower, or delay.)

## Pick ONE primary goal
Every launch optimizes for a single thing; name it and measure it:
- **Signups / leads** — top-of-funnel growth.
- **Awareness / traffic** — reach and brand.
- **Press / credibility** — third-party validation.
- **Activation** — get *existing* users onto the new thing.
Tie the goal to the metric tree. A launch chasing all four lands none.

## Resource to the tier
Don't spend tier-1 effort on a tier-3 release, or vice versa. The skill is calibration: the audience's attention is finite and you spend trust each time you ask for it — spend it where it pays.
