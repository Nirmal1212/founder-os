# Pre-launch — waitlist, referral, beta

Launch day fails when nobody's waiting. The pre-launch phase builds a warm audience primed to act the moment you go live, plus the proof you'll need on the day.

## Waitlist — why and how
- A waitlist converts strangers into a warm list you can activate on launch day, and signals demand (to you and to investors).
- Give a **real reason to join now**: early access, founding-member pricing, exclusive perks, or genuine scarcity. "Join our waitlist" with no benefit gets ignored.
- Capture the minimum (email; maybe one qualifying field) — every extra field drops conversion.
- Keep the list **warm**: occasional build-in-public updates, sneak peeks, behind-the-scenes. A cold waitlist forgets you by launch.

## Referral loop — turn waiters into recruiters
- Reward sharing: move up the list, unlock features/perks, or tiered rewards for N referrals (the classic mechanic that made several waitlists go viral).
- Make sharing one-tap with a personal referral link and a pre-written message.
- Only works if the underlying offer is genuinely wanted — referral mechanics amplify real demand, they don't manufacture it.

## Beta program — proof + product hardening
- A closed beta does double duty: hardens the product *and* produces the **testimonials, case studies, and metrics** you'll launch with.
- Recruit beta users who match the ICP (from research/context), not whoever's easiest.
- Instrument it: gather quotes, before/after outcomes, and usage proof you can cite on launch day (truthfully).
- Beta users become launch-day advocates — give them a reason to show up and share.

## Build-in-public (optional, founder-dependent)
- Sharing the journey (progress, decisions, metrics) builds an audience that's invested before launch.
- Fits founders comfortable being public; authenticity is the whole point — performative versions fall flat.

## The handoff to launch day
By the day you go live you should have: a warm waitlist to email, referral advocates ready to share, and real proof to show. That's the difference between a spike and a shrug.
