# Messaging house — one story, reused everywhere

The messaging house turns positioning into a reusable architecture so every channel tells the same story. Three layers: roof, pillars, foundation.

## The roof — the one-liner
The single sentence that says what it is and why it matters. Tests:
- A smart outsider gets it in one read — no jargon, no "leveraging synergies."
- It's specific to you (swap in a competitor → it should read false).
- It leads with value, not mechanism. ("Get paid on time, automatically" beats "AI-powered invoice workflow engine.")
Draft 5–10, kill the vague ones, keep the clearest. Clarity > cleverness — a clear line converts; a witty vague one confuses.

## The pillars — 3 value themes
Three benefits that hold up the one-liner. Rules:
- Phrased as **customer value**, not features ("Never chase a payment again", not "automated reminders").
- Distinct from each other — no overlap.
- Collectively cover why the best-fit customer buys.
Three is the target — two feels thin, five dilutes. Each pillar is a theme content and sales can expand on.

## The foundation — proof points
Under each pillar, the evidence that makes it believable:
- Features/capabilities that deliver the benefit.
- Hard proof: metrics, outcomes, customer results, integrations, security/compliance facts.
- Social proof: testimonials, logos, case studies (when you have them).
Claims without proof read as marketing fluff; proof is what makes a pillar credible.

## Using the house
- Homepage = one-liner + pillars + proof, top to bottom.
- Pitch deck, ads, sales one-pager, onboarding all pull from the *same* house.
- When messaging feels inconsistent across channels, it's because someone wrote outside the house — bring it back.

## Keep it honest
Never claim outcomes you can't support or invent testimonials/metrics. Under-promise in copy and let the product over-deliver — trust is the real conversion engine.
