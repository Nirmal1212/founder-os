# Market sizing — TAM / SAM / SOM

The point isn't a big number; it's a *defensible* number with visible assumptions, and the wedge you'll attack first.

## The three layers
- **TAM (Total Addressable Market)**: total annual revenue if every possible customer bought. The ceiling.
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**: the slice your product + business model + geography can actually serve today.
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**: what you can realistically capture in ~3 years given competition and go-to-market reality. This is the honest number.

## Compute it both ways
- **Top-down**: start from an industry figure (analyst report) and narrow by segment. Fast, but reports are broad, often inflated, and easy to misapply. Use as an *anchor and sanity check*, never as the answer.
- **Bottom-up** (preferred): `# of target customers × annual contract value`. Build from real, countable inputs — number of companies of type X, realistic price, realistic penetration. Forces you to confront the actual customer count.
- When the two diverge a lot, the bottom-up is usually closer to reality. Explain the gap rather than averaging.

## Show the assumptions
Every sizing is a chain of assumptions (customer count, adoption %, price, expansion). List them explicitly so they can be challenged. A SOM of $8M with five stated assumptions is more useful than a $30B TAM with none.

## For an early startup: the beachhead matters most
- The headline TAM is a slide; the **beachhead** — the first narrow segment you can dominate — is the strategy.
- A good beachhead is small enough to win, reachable through one channel, and a credible springboard to adjacent segments.
- Frame it: "We win [specific segment] first because [reachability + acute pain], then expand to [adjacent]."

## Red flags to avoid
- "1% of a huge market" reasoning — capturing 1% is not a plan.
- Counting customers who can't actually buy (wrong geography, can't afford, not your product).
- Static sizing — note whether the market is growing or shrinking; a growing SAM changes the story.
