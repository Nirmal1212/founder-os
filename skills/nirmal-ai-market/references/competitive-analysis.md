# Competitive analysis — teardown & gap matrix

Goal: not a catalog of everyone, but the **unoccupied, valuable position** you can take. Work from the customer's perspective — who solves their job today, and how well.

## Cast the net wide, then focus
Three kinds of competitor, all real:
- **Direct**: same solution to the same problem for the same buyer.
- **Indirect / substitute**: different solution to the same job (a different category that gets the job done).
- **Status quo / do-nothing**: the manual workaround — spreadsheet, email, internal tool, "we just live with it." Usually the *hardest* competitor and the most-forgotten. Always include it.

Map the whole field, then go deep on the 3–5 that actually shape the buyer's decision.

## Per-competitor teardown
For each key player capture: target customer, positioning/promise (their one-liner), core capabilities, pricing model + entry price, go-to-market motion, notable strengths, and **exploitable weaknesses** (where they underserve a segment or job). Pull live data — sites, pricing pages, reviews (G2/Reddit reveal real gripes).

## The positioning gap matrix
- Pick the **two axes the ICP actually cares about** (not generic "price vs. features" unless that's truly the decision). E.g. "depth for power users ↔ simplicity" × "team collaboration ↔ solo."
- Plot every competitor. The valuable output is the **empty quadrant that customers want** — the position you can own.
- If every quadrant is full, either the axes are wrong (pick ones that matter more) or differentiation must come from a different vector (segment, business model, experience).

## Jobs-based comparison beats feature grids
A 60-row feature matrix proves nothing — everyone checks most boxes. Compare on **how well each solves the customer's core job**, including non-feature factors: onboarding effort, trust, ecosystem, price fairness. That's where real differences live.

## Output
- The matrix (CSV) + a 3-part read: **the gap** (where to play), **the threat** (who's most dangerous and why), **the opening** (the wedge). Hand the gap to `nirmal-ai-positioning`.
