# Pricing benchmark — read the model, not just the number

When benchmarking competitors, the strategic insight is *how* they charge (model + value metric), not the dollar figure. The number is easy to copy; the model is the lever.

## Capture per competitor
- **Model**: per-seat, usage-based, tiered (good/better/best), flat, freemium, or hybrid.
- **Value metric**: the unit they charge on — seats, API calls, GB stored, contacts, transactions. This is the most important field.
- **Entry price & tiers**: lowest paid tier, what's gated, the jump between tiers.
- **Free offering**: free tier vs. trial vs. none, and what's deliberately limited to drive upgrade.
- **Hidden costs**: onboarding fees, overage rates, annual-only discounts.

## Why the value metric matters most
- A good value metric **scales with the value the customer gets**, so the bill feels fair as they grow (e.g. charging for outcomes/usage, not just seats).
- A misaligned value metric is an opening: if incumbents charge per-seat but value accrues per-usage, a usage-based entrant can feel fairer and win price-sensitive segments.
- Aligning your value metric to delivered value is a positioning weapon, not just a pricing detail.

## Reading the landscape for openings
- **Underserved tier**: everyone clusters at enterprise → room at the low/self-serve end (or vice versa).
- **Packaging confusion**: if competitors' pricing is opaque/complex, simple transparent pricing is a differentiator.
- **Value-metric mismatch**: charging on the wrong unit relative to value.

## Important boundary
This skill **benchmarks** — it surfaces models, metrics, and gaps. The actual pricing *decision* (what to charge, how to package) is made in `founder-os-positioning` (value-based framing) and `founder-os-sales` (the pricing conversation). Hand off the benchmark; don't set the price here.

## Data hygiene
Pricing pages change often — pull live, date the snapshot, cite the source. Note where pricing is "contact sales" (no public number) and infer the band from signals.
