# Positioning framework (Dunford's 5 components)

Positioning is the context you set so the right customer instantly gets what you are and why you're the obvious choice. It's deliberate, relative, and built from truth — not a tagline.

## The five components (work in order)
1. **Competitive alternatives** — what the customer would do/use if your product didn't exist. Often the status quo (a spreadsheet, a manual process), sometimes a direct competitor. This is the *frame* — everything else is defined relative to it.
2. **Unique attributes** — the capabilities/qualities you have that the alternatives lack. Must be objectively true and demonstrable. Pull from the product and the market teardown.
3. **Value (and proof)** — what those attributes *enable* for the customer, in their terms. Each attribute → "so what?" → a concrete benefit. This is what they actually buy.
4. **Best-fit customers** — the segment for whom that value is most compelling and urgent. The people who care *most* about exactly what makes you different.
5. **Market category** — the context you frame yourself in, which triggers a set of expectations and a comparison set. Choosing the category is strategic: pick the frame where your unique attributes are the *deciding* criteria.

## The logic chain
Alternatives → (vs. them) Unique attributes → (which deliver) Value → (most wanted by) Best-fit customers → (understood within) Category. If any link is weak, the positioning collapses.

## Positioning statement (one synthesized output)
A compact statement, e.g.:
> For [best-fit customer] who [need/situation], [product] is a [category] that [key value] — unlike [alternative], we [unique attribute/why].

Keep it specific. If you could swap in a competitor's name and it still reads true, it's not positioning yet.

## Common failure modes
- **Positioning in a vacuum** — no named alternative, so "better" means nothing.
- **Aspirational attributes** — claiming differentiation you can't prove.
- **Feature-listing** — stopping at attributes, never reaching value.
- **Positioning for everyone** — no best-fit segment, so it lands with no one.
- **Defaulting to the obvious category** — sometimes a different frame makes your strengths the deciding factor; choosing the category is a lever, not a given.
