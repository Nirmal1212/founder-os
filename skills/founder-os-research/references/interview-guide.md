# Writing a non-leading interview guide

The interview is an instrument; a biased instrument gives biased data permanently. Structure for honest signal.

## Structure of a generative interview (~45 min)
1. **Warm-up** (5m): context, role, a typical day. Builds rapport, sets the frame.
2. **The last time** (20m): walk through the most recent time they faced the problem. Concrete, recalled, real.
3. **Probe the job** (10m): what were they trying to accomplish, what did they try, what was hard, what did they do instead (workarounds).
4. **Magnitude** (5m): how often, how much it costs them, what they've spent/tried to solve it.
5. **Open** (5m): "what didn't I ask that I should have?"

## The cardinal rule — past behavior, not hypotheticals
- Ask: "Tell me about the last time you…" / "Walk me through how you…"
- Not: "Would you use…" / "Do you think you'd like…" — these produce polite, useless predictions.

## Non-leading phrasing
- Bad (leads): "How frustrating is the current export process?" → assumes frustration.
- Good (neutral): "Tell me about the last time you exported data. What happened?"
- Don't pitch your solution and ask if they like it — you'll get social-desirability bias. Learn the problem; reveal nothing about the answer you hope for.

## Dig with "why" and silence
- Follow vague answers with "why was that?" / "what do you mean by…" up to ~5 levels.
- Let silence sit — people fill it with the real answer.

## Watch for
- **The mom test**: facts about their life are data; compliments and opinions about your idea are noise.
- **Hypothetical drift**: when they slip into "I would probably…", steer back to "last time, what did you actually do?"
- Record exact language — their words become the product's and the marketing's vocabulary.
