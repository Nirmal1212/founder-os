# SEO playbook — intent over volume

SEO done right is matching content to what a searcher is actually trying to do, structured so search engines see you as an authority on a topic. Volume is a vanity input; intent and fit are what convert.

## Search intent (classify every target keyword)
- **Informational**: learning ("how to do X", "what is Y") — TOFU/MOFU.
- **Commercial**: comparing/evaluating ("best X tools", "X vs Y") — MOFU, high-value.
- **Transactional**: ready to act ("X pricing", "buy X", "X alternative") — BOFU.
- **Navigational**: looking for a specific brand.
Match the keyword's intent to a funnel stage and the right content type. A high-volume informational term with no buyer behind it can be worth less than a low-volume commercial term your ICP searches.

## Topic clusters (the structure that wins)
- **Pillar page**: a comprehensive page on a broad topic you want to own.
- **Cluster posts**: focused pieces on subtopics, each linking up to the pillar (and the pillar linking down).
This internal-linking structure signals topical authority — far more effective than scattered one-off posts chasing unrelated keywords.

## Content briefs (what you hand a writer / yourself)
Each brief: target keyword + intent, the searcher's underlying job, the angle (your unique take), a working outline, internal links (to pillar + related), and the CTA matched to stage. A brief turns "write something about X" into an executable, on-strategy piece.

## On-page essentials
- Title + H1 match intent and include the term naturally.
- Answer the query *fast* (don't bury the answer under 800 words of preamble).
- Scannable structure (headers, lists where natural), one clear CTA.
- Genuinely better/more useful than what currently ranks — that's the actual ranking factor over time.

## E-E-A-T / trust
Demonstrate experience and expertise (real know-how, original insight, accurate facts). Don't publish thin, generic, or fabricated content — it doesn't rank and it erodes trust. Cite real sources; never invent stats to fill a brief.

## The honest caveat
SEO compounds slowly (months). It's a durable channel, not a launch tactic — pair it with faster channels (social, community, launch) for near-term reach.
