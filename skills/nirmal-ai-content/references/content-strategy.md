# Content strategy — the engine, not the output

Strategy decides what's worth making and how it gets seen. Without it, content is a treadmill that produces motion and no traction.

## Pillars — themes you'll own
Pick 3–5 themes tied to the positioning value pillars and the ICP's real questions. You want to be *the* place for these topics, not a dabbler in everything. Each pillar is a territory you build authority in over time.

## Funnel mapping (TOFU / MOFU / BOFU)
Content serves different jobs at different stages:
- **TOFU (awareness)**: the problem space, the shift, education. Broad reach, no hard sell. ("Why X is getting harder.")
- **MOFU (consideration)**: approaches, comparisons, frameworks, how-tos. The reader is evaluating ways to solve it. ("How teams solve X / X vs Y approaches.")
- **BOFU (decision)**: your product, proof, case studies, comparisons vs. named alternatives. ("How [product] does X.")
Most early startups overload BOFU and starve TOFU — then wonder why no one's in the funnel. Balance toward the top to fill it.

## Channels — where the ICP actually is
Pick the 1–3 channels the ICP genuinely uses (a specific search behavior, LinkedIn, a niche community, a newsletter). Don't spread thin across every platform. One channel done well beats five done badly.

## Cadence — sustainable beats heroic
A rhythm you can hold for a year (e.g. one substantial piece + repurposing weekly) beats a burst that burns out in a month. Consistency compounds; sporadic doesn't.

## Distribution-first
Before committing to make something, answer: how will it be seen? If there's no distribution plan, the piece is a gamble. Plan the channel, the hook, and the repurposing *before* writing.

## Tie to metrics
Each pillar/stage should map to a goal from the metric tree (awareness → traffic; consideration → signups; decision → activation). Content with no metric attached is hard to justify or improve.
