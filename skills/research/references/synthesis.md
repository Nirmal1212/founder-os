# Synthesis — from raw input to insight

Synthesis is where research creates value or invents fiction. Discipline: build up from observations; never start from the conclusion you wanted.

## The bottom-up flow
1. **Observations** — atomic, concrete things said or done, each tagged to a participant (P1, P2…). No interpretation yet.
2. **Themes** — cluster observations that share meaning (affinity mapping). Let clusters emerge; don't pre-name them.
3. **Jobs-to-be-done** — for each durable need, frame it: *"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."* JTBD survives feature churn; "they asked for a button" does not.
4. **Insight statements** — pair an observation with its implication: *"[What we saw] → [so what for the product]."*

## Confidence — label every insight
State strength so the reader weights it correctly:
- **How many**: 1 of 6 vs. 5 of 6.
- **Consistency**: did it recur or was it a one-off?
- **Said vs. observed**: behavioral evidence > stated preference.
- Tag each insight Strong / Moderate / Weak. A vivid single quote is a *hypothesis*, not a finding — say so.

## Signal vs. noise
- A loud outlier is tempting and usually unrepresentative. Weight by frequency and consistency, not vividness.
- Actively record **disconfirming** evidence — the cases that cut against the emerging story. Hiding them is how teams talk themselves into bad bets.

## Separate three things, visibly
- **Observation** (what happened) — **Inference** (what you think it means) — **Recommendation** (what to do). Keep them distinct so the logic is auditable and the user can disagree at the right layer.

## Output of synthesis
- A short theme list, the JTBD set, insight statements with confidence, and the **open questions / what to learn next**. Feed the durable parts (ICP, personas, validated jobs) back to `context`.
