# Sales collateral — assets that sell in the room (and out of it)

Collateral does sales work when you're not there. All of it pulls from the messaging house so the story is consistent with the site, deck, and pitch.

## One-pager (the leave-behind)
The single page a champion forwards to the rest of the buying committee. Must sell on its own:
- **Headline**: the one-liner (value-first, from positioning).
- **Problem**: the pain in the buyer's words.
- **How it works**: 3 value pillars, each as a benefit + the proof.
- **Proof**: a metric, a logo/testimonial, a result.
- **CTA**: one clear next step (book a demo, start a trial).
Keep it to one page, scannable in 30 seconds — it competes with a full inbox.

## Demo script (story-driven, not a feature tour)
- **Open on their problem**, not your UI. Restate what you learned in discovery so it's about them.
- **Show the path to value** — the shortest route to the "aha", not every feature. Tailor to what *this* buyer cares about.
- **Narrate outcomes**: "so now, instead of X taking a day, it's done here in a click."
- **End on the outcome + next step.** Always close a demo with a proposed action.
- Common failure: the feature-tour demo that shows everything and lands nothing. Show less, mean more.

## Battle cards (for the team)
Internal quick-reference for competitive deals:
- Per competitor: their pitch, where you win, where they win (be honest), and the trap to avoid.
- Objection → response pairs (see sales-motion).
- **Honesty matters**: a battle card that pretends you win everything gets reps caught flat-footed. Acknowledge real strengths and redirect to where you genuinely differ.

## Keep collateral current and consistent
- Everything sources from the messaging house and real proof. When positioning changes, collateral updates — stale collateral contradicts the live pitch.
- Never put unsupported claims or invented logos/quotes on collateral; it's the most-scrutinized, most-forwarded material you have.
