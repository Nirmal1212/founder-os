# Pitch narrative — story, not feature tour

A pitch persuades by telling a story the buyer sees themselves in, then asking for a clear next step. Slides are the story's beats; cut anything that doesn't move toward the ask.

## The arc
1. **Problem / the shift** — open in the buyer's world: the pain, made vivid, and the "why now" (from the positioning narrative). They should nod before you mention your product.
2. **Solution** — your product as the answer to *that* problem. Show the outcome and the "after" state, not a feature inventory.
3. **Why you / why now** — the unique insight, unfair advantage, or timing that makes you the right answer and now the right moment.
4. **Proof** — traction, results, named customers, testimonials, metrics. Truthful and specific. This is what converts skeptics.
5. **The ask** — one clear next step. Don't end on "questions?"; end on "here's what I propose we do next."

## Lead with them, not you
The weakest pitches open with "we're a company that…". The strongest open with the buyer's problem. Earn the right to talk about yourself by first showing you understand their world.

## Sales deck ≠ investor deck (keep separate)
- **Sales deck**: audience = buyer; ask = trial/pilot/purchase; proof = it solves *their* problem; ROI-focused.
- **Investor deck**: audience = investor; ask = the raise; proof = market size, growth, team, why this is venture-scale.
A single deck trying to do both confuses both. Build two.

## Show, don't list
Wherever possible, replace a feature bullet with the outcome it produces or a concrete before/after. "Cut close time from 5 days to 5 hours" beats "automated reconciliation engine."

## Tighten
A pitch is as strong as its weakest slide. If a slide doesn't advance problem → solution → proof → ask, cut it. Fewer, sharper slides beat a comprehensive deck nobody remembers.
