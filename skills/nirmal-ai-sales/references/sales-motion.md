# Sales motion — discovery, qualification, objections, pricing

Early sales is discovery in disguise: every call teaches you about the buyer and the product. Win by understanding and qualifying, not by talking.

## Discovery (ask more than you pitch)
The best early calls are ~70% listening. Understand before presenting:
- **Problem**: "Walk me through how you handle X today. Where does it break down?"
- **Impact**: "What does that cost you — time, money, missed opportunities?"
- **Current solution**: "What have you tried? Why hasn't it stuck?"
- **Process**: "If this works, what happens next? Who else is involved in the decision?"
Past-behavior and specifics, not hypotheticals (same discipline as user research). Discovery both qualifies the deal and feeds product learning.

## Qualification (disqualify fast)
Use a simple frame and apply it honestly:
- **Need** — is there a real, urgent problem you solve?
- **Budget** — can they pay (now or realistically soon)?
- **Authority** — are you talking to (or can you reach) the decision-maker?
- **Timeline** — is there a reason to act, or is it "someday"?
Chasing bad-fit deals is the #1 time sink in early sales. A fast, clean "this isn't a fit" is a win — it frees you for real opportunities and spares the buyer.

## Objection handling (objections are information)
1. **Acknowledge** — don't get defensive.
2. **Understand** — "Help me understand what's behind that" — get to the *real* concern (price objections are often value or trust concerns).
3. **Respond with evidence** — proof, a reframe, or a relevant example.
4. **Confirm** — "Does that address it?"
Never steamroll. An objection met with curiosity builds trust; one bulldozed ends the deal quietly.
Common: "too expensive" (→ anchor on value/ROI), "no time to switch" (→ onboarding ease, cost of staying), "need feature X" (→ is it a dealbreaker or a nice-to-have?), "happy with current tool" (→ what would have to be true to change?).

## The pricing conversation
- **Anchor on value, not cost**: tie price to the cost of the problem / the ROI delivered, not your expenses.
- Use the **market benchmark** (from `nirmal-ai-market`) for context on models and ranges.
- **Don't reflexively discount** — a quick discount signals the original price was fiction and trains the buyer to push. If you flex, get something back (longer term, case study, referral).
- For early deals, treat price as **learning**: discover what this segment will actually pay; that informs the real pricing decision.
- Be willing to walk. Desperation closes bad deals that churn.

## Honesty as strategy
No manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, or overpromising. Early-stage reputation is everything and buyers talk. The deal that closes on a lie churns and damages the brand; the honest "no" keeps the door open.
